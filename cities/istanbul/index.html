Istanbul Türkiye

City 12 of 15 · Asia

Fly from the Galata Tower across the water to Asia.

The hills drop to the water. Get past the simit gull, then Karagöz.

The city

The city on two continents

Greek settlers founded Byzantium on the Bosphorus around 660 BC. In 330 the Roman emperor Constantine made it his capital, Constantinople. The Ottomans took the city in 1453 and ruled an empire from it for nearly five centuries. Today Istanbul lies on two continents, with Europe on one shore of the strait and Asia on the other.

“I'd fly from Europe to Asia in one throw.”

The landmark

The Galata Tower

Genoese merchants built the stone Galata Tower in 1348, on the hill above the Golden Horn. The Ottomans later used it to watch for fires across the wooden city. The writer Evliya Çelebi tells that around 1630 a man named Hezarfen Ahmed Çelebi jumped from its top on home-made wings and glided across the Bosphorus.

Two keepers

Who keeps the wish in Istanbul.

An Elite wakes first; the Boss guards the wish itself. Hit their weak points for a BONK!, land the last blow for a KO!, and each one grins and takes a bow.

  • The Elite

    The Simit Gull

    Never paid for a simit · Keeper of the Bosphorus ferries

    Gulls follow Istanbul's ferries across the Bosphorus every day, and passengers throw them pieces of simit, the sesame bread rings sold from street carts. The boldest birds catch them in mid-air, and some take them straight from people's hands.

    The gull lifts its head, simit in its beak, and gives the plane a sideways look. The gull squawks and loses a feather, but keeps hold of the simit. The gull bows. The flock snatches the simit, and it stares into its empty beak.

    “I'd save it a piece of mine.”

  • The Boss

    Karagöz

    Always has the last word · Keeper of the Golden Horn

    Karagöz is the loud, plain-spoken hero of Turkish shadow theatre. In every play he argues with his learned friend Hacivat, and he usually gets the last laugh. In 2009 the Karagöz tradition joined UNESCO's list of the world's living heritage.

    Karagöz raises one finger, clears his throat and starts to argue with the plane. Karagöz staggers, grabs his hat and keeps talking. Karagöz stops talking at last, sweeps off his hat and bows low.

    “I'd let him have the last word.”
