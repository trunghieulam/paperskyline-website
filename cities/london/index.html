The city

From Londinium

The Romans founded Londinium around AD 47 beside a bridge over the Thames. In 1666 the Great Fire burned most of the old walled city in four days, and Christopher Wren rebuilt 51 of its churches. By the 1820s London was the largest city in the world.

“I'd cross the Thames where the Romans built their bridge.”

The landmark

The great clock tower

The clock tower at the Houses of Parliament was finished in 1859 and renamed the Elizabeth Tower in 2012. Big Ben is really the name of its great bell. To keep the clock right, its keepers stack old pennies on the pendulum: each one speeds it up by 0.4 seconds a day.

    The Tower Raven

    Fed and counted every day · Keeper of the Tower of London

    Legend says that if the ravens ever leave the Tower of London, the Crown will fall, so at least six are always kept there, with one to spare, cared for by a Ravenmaster. A tale credits Charles II with the rule, but no one can prove it, and some historians think the legend is much younger.

    “I'd count them twice, just to be sure.”

  • The Boss

    The Guildhall Giant

    The City's oldest guardian · Keeper of the Guildhall

    Old tales say giants guarded Britain before London was founded, and carved giants have led the Lord Mayor's procession for centuries. The Guildhall's pair, carved in 1708, burned in the Blitz of December 1940; new figures were carved in 1953 and still stand in the Guildhall, ready to march out again.

    Steam hisses. The clock in the giant's chest starts ticking again. The giant rocks back a step. The clock in his chest skips, then ticks on. The giant lowers his spear, and his clock stops at the hour he woke.

    “I'd ask him what London looked like at the start.”

    Guards a relic: the Crown Jewels Sceptre. The sceptre of the Crown Jewels, kept in the Tower and watched over by the Guildhall Giant. It gives each strike the weight of a crown.
