The city

Where Paris began

More than 2,000 years ago a Celtic people, the Parisii, lived where the Seine was easy to cross. The Romans took their town in 52 BC and called it Lutetia. It spread over both banks and the islands between, and in the end the tribe's name stuck: Paris.

“I'd find the old river crossing and start there.”

The landmark

The iron tower

Gustave Eiffel's company built the tower for the 1889 World's Fair, and for 41 years it was the tallest structure people had ever made. It was meant to come down after twenty years. Radio saved it: the tower made a perfect aerial, so it stayed.

Two keepers

Who keeps the wish in Paris.

  • The Elite

    The Carousel Horse

    Gallops all day and never leaves · Keeper of the Trocadéro

    In June 1662 Louis XIV threw a horseback festival beside the Louvre so grand that the square is still called the Place du Carrousel. Riders galloped to spear a hanging ring with a lance. Tradition says wooden horses on a turning platform soon let anyone try, and the carousel was born.

    The carousel horse opens one painted eye. The horse stumbles, then finds its gallop again. The horse rears once and comes to rest. The music stops.

    “I'd ride the golden one and catch the ring.”

  • The Boss

    The Stryge

    The chimera of Notre-Dame · Keeper of the Île de la Cité

    Le Stryge looks medieval but is younger: Viollet-le-Duc had the brooding chimera set on Notre-Dame's high gallery during his restoration, begun in the 1840s. The etcher Charles Méryon made it famous in 1853 and named it after the strix, a night bird of Roman legend.

    High on Notre-Dame's gallery, the Stryge lifts its chin and turns to watch the plane. The Stryge flinches, then goes back to watching. The Stryge folds its wings, rests its chin in its hands and goes still.

    “I'd sit beside him and watch the city.”
