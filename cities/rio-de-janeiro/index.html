The city

The River of January

Portuguese sailors entered its bay in January 1502 and, taking it for a river mouth, named it Rio de Janeiro, "River of January". From 1808 the Portuguese court ruled its empire from here, the only time a European capital sat outside Europe. It stayed Brazil's capital until 1960.

“I'd sail into the bay and mistake it too.”

The landmark

Sugarloaf Mountain

Sugarloaf is a 396-metre dome of granite and quartz rising straight from the water at the mouth of Guanabara Bay. Its name most likely comes from the cone-shaped clay moulds sugar was shipped in, back when Brazil sold sugar to the world. Climbers still scale its bare rock walls.

    The Cable Car

    Hangs on by one wire · Keeper of Sugarloaf

    When it opened in 1912, the Sugarloaf cable car was, by most counts, only the third passenger cableway in the world, after ones in Spain and Switzerland. Engineer Augusto Ferreira Ramos dreamed it up in 1908. Its first cars were wooden, like Rio's trams, and carried 22 people through the air above Guanabara Bay.

    The cable car sways on its wire. The cable car swings hard and rattles on its wire. The cable car stops swinging and hangs still.

    “I'd sit by the window and never blink.”

  • The Boss

    The Samba Dancer

    Never misses a beat · Keeper of the Sambadrome

    Deixa Falar, "Let Them Talk", often called the first samba school, was founded in Rio's Estácio neighbourhood in 1928 by musicians including Ismael Silva. Since 1984 the schools have paraded through Oscar Niemeyer's Sambadrome, each with thousands of dancers, drummers and towering floats.

    “I'd dance the whole parade and never stop.”
