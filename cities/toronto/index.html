The city

Trees standing in the water

Toronto's name probably comes from the Mohawk word tkaronto, "where there are trees standing in the water". The British founded the town of York here in 1793, and it became the city of Toronto in 1834. Today it is Canada's largest city, and almost half of its people were born in another country.

“Half the city came from somewhere else. I'd ask them where.”

The landmark

The CN Tower

The CN Tower was finished in 1976 to carry TV and radio signals over the city's new skyscrapers. At 553 metres it was the world's tallest free-standing structure for more than thirty years. Visitors can stand on its glass floor, 342 metres up, and look straight down.

Two keepers

Who keeps the wish in Toronto.

An Elite wakes first; the Boss guards the wish itself. Hit their weak points for a BONK!, land the last blow for a KO!, and each one grins and takes a bow.

  • The Elite

    The Goalie Moose

    Nothing gets past these antlers · Keeper of the outdoor rink

    Every winter Toronto floods parks and squares to make outdoor ice rinks, and children learn to skate and play hockey on them. The moose is the tallest animal in North America: a big one stands over two metres at the shoulder and weighs up to 700 kilograms.

    The moose crouches in its pads, taps its stick twice and whispers sorry in advance. The moose's antlers wobble, and it says sorry, though the plane hit it. The moose takes off its mask, bows, and gives a polite wave.

    “I'd bring my own puck and say sorry first.”

  • The Boss

    The Raccoon Night-Watchman

    Opens every lid in the city · Keeper of the laneways

    Toronto has so many raccoons that people call it the raccoon capital of the world. Their clever paws can open latches and lids. In 2016 the city gave out new green bins with locking lids, and some raccoons learned to open those too.

    The raccoon clicks on its flashlight and jangles its keys. Someone is out after dark. The raccoon jumps, and its ring of keys jangles. The raccoon lifts a bin lid like a hat and bows.

    “I'd keep my snacks in my pocket.”
