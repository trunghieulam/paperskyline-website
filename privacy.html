At a glance

  • Paper Skyline is a single-player game made by THLab (Trung Hieu Lam). This policy covers the app on iOS and Android.
  • The game itself collects nothing about you. There is no account, no sign-in and nothing to register.
  • No analytics or crash reporting, and no in-app purchases.
  • On Android you can choose to watch a short ad from Google AdMob for spare fuel. No ad is shown unless you tap to watch one, though Google's ad software starts at launch to check consent and load one in the background. Every ad is non-personalised and treated as child-directed. The iPhone and iPad version has no ads.
  • Your progress and your settings are saved on your own device and stay there.
  • Paris comes with the app. New York, Tokyo, Cairo and Rio de Janeiro are downloaded once over HTTPS. The download sends no account, advertising ID or device identifier.
  • Deleting the app deletes everything. There is no copy anywhere else.

What stays on your device

Paper Skyline keeps three kinds of data in the app's own private storage on your phone or tablet. None of it is ever sent anywhere.

City downloads

Paris is inside the app and plays offline from the first launch. The next four cities, New York, Tokyo, Cairo and Rio de Janeiro, are downloaded from our file storage on Cloudflare R2 the first time you need them. After that each city plays offline too.

  • When the game starts with a connection, it fetches a short list of the city files available for this version of the app.
  • On Wi-Fi, reaching a city fetches the next one in the background. On mobile data the game shows the size and asks before downloading.
  • Every request uses HTTPS and asks only for a file. It carries no account, name, advertising ID, device identifier, save data, or anything about how you play.

Like any request on the internet, it reaches the server with your device's IP address and standard technical details, such as the engine's name, so that the file can be sent back. Cloudflare handles that delivery under its own privacy policy. We do not use those details to identify, profile or track anyone, and we do not combine them with anything else.

The optional ad

On Android, the garage and the landing card offer spare fuel with a small “or watch an ad” link. Tapping it plays one rewarded video ad from Google AdMob. Nothing in the game requires it, and no ad appears unless you tap. You can watch at most three an hour and ten a day, at least ten minutes apart.

  • Never personalised. Every ad request asks Google for non-personalised ads and is marked as child-directed, for every player, whatever their age. Google does not use it for personalised advertising or remarketing.
  • Consent first in the EEA and the UK. Before any ad loads there, the game shows Google's consent form. The form appears again whenever your choice needs renewing, and uninstalling the app resets it.
  • What Google receives. To serve and measure the ad, Google's ad software (the Google Mobile Ads SDK, with Google's User Messaging Platform for consent) collects your IP address, which gives an approximate location; interactions such as app launches, taps and video views; and diagnostics such as launch time, hang rate and energy use. It may also send the app set ID, an identifier shared only by apps from the same developer on your device. Google uses this for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and it is encrypted in transit. The game does not declare the advertising ID permission and never reads your advertising ID.
  • When it connects. On Android the ad software starts when the game launches: it checks consent, then loads one ad in the background so it is ready if you tap. Loading an ad contacts Google even if you never watch it. Apart from Google's ad and consent servers, the game connects only to our city file storage.

Google handles that data under its own policy: how Google uses information from apps that use its services. We never receive anything that identifies you, only totals such as how many ads were watched.

What it does not collect

To be specific, because vagueness in a privacy policy is usually hiding something. Apart from the optional ad above, Paper Skyline does not collect, transmit, sell or share:

  • your name, email address, phone number or any other contact detail;
  • your location, precise or approximate;
  • photographs, the camera, the microphone, or your contacts;
  • your device's advertising identifier, or any other identifier used to track you across apps;
  • analytics, usage statistics, crash reports or diagnostics;
  • your progress, your save data or what happens in your flights, to Google or anyone else.

There is nothing to buy in Paper Skyline. If that changes, or the ad changes, this policy is rewritten and published before the change reaches a player, never after.

Permissions

The game never shows a permission prompt. On Android it declares network access and network state, which it uses for the city downloads and the optional ad, and to tell Wi-Fi from mobile data. Google's ad software adds two more, to keep the device awake and to run a foreground service while it works. It does not declare the advertising ID permission. It does not use the camera, the microphone, your location, your files or your contacts. If something claiming to be Paper Skyline asks for one of these, it is not this game.

The engine

Paper Skyline is built with Unity. Unity offers optional cloud services, such as analytics, crash reporting, advertising and player accounts, and none of them is switched on in this game. The only third-party software in the game that collects data is Google's ad software, described in The optional ad.

Apple and Google

When you install any app, the store you installed it from keeps its own records of that: the download itself, and, if you have that turned on, crash reports collected by the operating system. Those records belong to Apple and Google and are governed by their privacy policies, not this one. We receive from them only anonymous, aggregated counts, such as how many people installed the game, which tell us nothing about any individual.

Children

Paper Skyline can be played by children and follows Google Play's Families policy. The game itself collects no personal information from anyone: there are no accounts, no chat and nothing to buy, and the city downloads send no identifiers. The only ad is one the player chooses to watch, and every ad request is marked child-directed and non-personalised, so it is never used for personalised advertising or remarketing. Google's ad software is self-certified for Google Play's Families policy when set up this way. A parent who wants the game's data gone can use Reset everything in Settings, or delete the app.
