At a glance

  • Paper Skyline is a single-player game made by THLab (Trung Hieu Lam). This policy covers the Android app and the feedback form on this website. An iPhone and iPad version may follow, and this policy will be updated when it does.
  • The game collects no personal information. There is no account, no sign-in and nothing to register.
  • The game sends anonymous gameplay statistics, such as how far a flight went and which upgrades were bought, so that we can balance it. They carry no identifier, and you can switch them off in Settings.
  • No crash reporting and no in-app purchases.
  • On Android you can choose to watch a short ad from Google AdMob for bonus cash. No ad is shown unless you tap to watch one, though Google's ad software starts at launch to check consent and load one in the background. Every ad is non-personalised and treated as child-directed.
  • Your progress and your settings are saved on your own device and stay there.
  • Paris comes with the app. The other nine cities, from New York to San Francisco, are downloaded once over HTTPS. The download sends no account, advertising ID or device identifier.
  • Deleting the app deletes everything on your device. Anonymous statistics already sent are kept for up to 12 months, then deleted.

City downloads

Paris is inside the app and plays offline from the first launch. The next four cities, New York, Tokyo, Cairo and Rio de Janeiro, are downloaded from our file storage on Cloudflare R2 the first time you need them. After that each city plays offline too.

  • When the game starts with a connection, it fetches a short list of the city files available for this version of the app, and a short list of game news.
  • On Android, tapping Update on a news entry opens Google Play's own update screen, through Google's in-app update library, which talks to the Play Store app on your device.
  • On Wi-Fi, reaching a city fetches the next one in the background. On mobile data the game shows the size and asks before downloading.
  • Every request uses HTTPS and asks only for a file. It carries no account, name, advertising ID, device identifier, save data, or anything about how you play.

Like any request on the internet, it reaches the server with your device's IP address and standard technical details, such as the engine's name, so that the file can be sent back. Cloudflare handles that delivery under its own privacy policy. We do not use those details to identify, profile or track anyone, and we do not combine them with anything else.

The optional ad

On Android, the landing card after a flight offers bonus cash with a “Watch an ad” button. Tapping it plays one rewarded video ad from Google AdMob. Nothing in the game requires it, and no ad appears unless you tap. You can watch at most three an hour and ten a day, at least ten minutes apart. If an iPhone and iPad version follows, this section will be updated to say whether it shows ads.

  • Never personalised. Every ad request asks Google for non-personalised ads and is marked as child-directed, for every player, whatever their age. Google does not use it for personalised advertising or remarketing. Google's ad software is self-certified for Google Play's Families policy when set up this way.
  • Consent first in the EEA and the UK. Before any ad loads there, the game shows Google's consent form. To change your choice later, open Settings and tap Ad privacy choices; that entry appears wherever Google requires it.
  • What Google receives. To serve and measure the ad, Google's ad software (the Google Mobile Ads SDK, with Google's User Messaging Platform for consent) collects your IP address, which gives an approximate location; interactions such as app launches, taps and video views; and diagnostics such as launch time, hang rate and energy use. It may also send the app set ID, an identifier shared only by apps from the same developer on your device. Google uses this for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and it is encrypted in transit. The game does not declare the advertising ID permission and never reads your advertising ID.
  • When it connects. On Android the ad software starts when the game launches: it checks consent, then loads one ad in the background so it is ready if you tap. Loading an ad contacts Google even if you never watch it. Apart from Google's ad and consent servers, the game connects only to our city file storage and our statistics server.

Google handles that data under its own policy: how Google uses information from apps that use its services. We never receive anything that identifies you, only totals such as how many ads were watched.

Gameplay statistics

So that we can tune prices, upgrades and cities from real play, the game sends short, anonymous records of what happens in it. For now only the Android version sends them, and only copies installed from the store. Nothing is recorded before your first flight has landed or finished.

  • In every record. The game version, the date (UTC, with no time of day), the city, the difficulty, which flight of this city visit it was, how many flights you have flown in total, and your purchase history so far: each upgrade with its level and the flight you bought it on, plus the cities you moved on to.
  • After each flight. Your upgrade levels, what the flight paid (for distance, height, time, speed, things knocked over and coins, and in total), the coins you then held, whether it landed or finished, how far and how high it went and how long it was in the air, seconds of engine, your strikes on each keeper, glancing hits, and the relics you wore.
  • Leaving the garage. Your coins before and after, the upgrades you could afford, the garage's suggestion, what you bought, in order, and the relics you wore.
  • Milestones. An Elite or a Boss broken, a relic or a relic slot won, a city cleared, or bonus cash earned from an ad, with which keeper it was.
  • Leaving the game. Which screen was open, a flight or the garage, when the game was closed from the background. It is sent the next time you open the game.
  • City content. From Paper Skyline 1.2: which version of each downloaded city's content is on your device, and whether it was just downloaded or already saved (for example v3:1a2b3c4d:cache). It says which content you have, not who you are.
  • Never sent. No account, install, device, advertising or app set ID; no time of day and no free text.
  • Not added. The game adds no device model, operating-system version, language or region to the statistics; each batch carries only the game version and the records. Like every web request, a batch arrives with the IP address and the standard device details, such as the phone model and Android version, and our server does not keep them.

The records are not linked to any person or device. Records from the same player share the same purchase history, which is how we see the order in which players upgrade.

  • Where they go. Over HTTPS to our own small server, a Cloudflare Worker at paperskyline-stats.paperskyline-stats.workers.dev, which stores them in a Cloudflare D1 database. Cloudflare processes them for us. Our server never reads or stores your IP address, and its request logs are off. To limit abuse, Cloudflare's rate counter holds a scrambled code made from the IP address and the date, which is never written down.
  • How long. A daily clean-up deletes records older than 12 months.
  • Switching off. Statistics are on by default. Turn off Share gameplay stats in Settings and the game stops sending and deletes any records not yet sent.

We use the records only to balance Paper Skyline. We do not sell or share them, and never combine them with anything else.

Feedback

  • In the game. The landing card and Send feedback in Settings offer five choices: Too hard, Too easy, Confusing, Loved it and Found a bug. There is no box to type into. Your choice and where you picked it are sent with the fields every record carries, to the same server, and kept the same way. It is recorded only while Share gameplay stats is on, and never during a replay.
  • Ratings. After your first city is cleared, the game may ask Google Play once to show its rating sheet. Google Play decides whether to show it, and any rating goes to Google; the game never sees it.
  • On this website. The feedback form opens your email app, so your message and any reply address reach us by email. We use them only to answer you, never share them, and delete them when they are no longer needed.

What it does not collect

To be specific, because vagueness in a privacy policy is usually hiding something. Apart from the optional ad and the anonymous statistics above, Paper Skyline does not collect, transmit, sell or share:

  • your name, email address, phone number or any other contact detail;
  • your location, precise or approximate;
  • photographs, the camera, the microphone, or your contacts;
  • your device's advertising identifier, or any other identifier used to track you across apps;
  • crash reports, or any statistics beyond the ones described above;
  • your save file itself, to anyone.

There is nothing to buy in Paper Skyline. If that changes, or the ad changes, this policy is rewritten and published before the change reaches a player, never after.

Permissions

The game never shows a permission prompt. On Android it declares network access and network state, which it uses for the city downloads and the optional ad, and to tell Wi-Fi from mobile data. Google's ad software adds two more, to keep the device awake and to run a foreground service while it works. It does not declare the advertising ID permission or any of Android's ad-services permissions. It does not use the camera, the microphone, your location, your files or your contacts. If something claiming to be Paper Skyline asks for one of these, it is not this game.

The engine

Paper Skyline is built with Unity. Unity offers optional cloud services, such as analytics, crash reporting, advertising and player accounts, and none of them is switched on in this game. Its hardware statistics reporting is off too, and the game contacts no Unity server. The only third-party software in the game that collects data is Google's ad software, described in The optional ad. The gameplay statistics go to our own server, not to an analytics company.

Apple and Google

When you install any app, the store you installed it from keeps its own records of that: the download itself, and, if you have that turned on, crash reports collected by the operating system. Those records belong to Apple and Google and are governed by their privacy policies, not this one. We receive from them only anonymous, aggregated counts, such as how many people installed the game, which tell us nothing about any individual.

Children

Paper Skyline is made for players 16 and over (or the age of digital consent where you live, if that is higher) and is not directed at anyone younger. The game collects no personal information from anyone: there are no accounts, no chat and nothing to buy, and the city downloads and gameplay statistics send no identifiers. Every ad request is marked child-directed and non-personalised. If a child has played it anyway, a parent who wants their data gone can use Reset everything in Settings, switch the statistics off, or delete the app. Records already sent carry no identifier, so no one can pick out a single player's records to delete; they are deleted after 12 months. Questions about a child's privacy go to the address under Contact.
